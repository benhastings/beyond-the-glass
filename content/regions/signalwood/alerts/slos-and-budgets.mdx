<Step id="budget" title="A budget for failure">

An <Term id="slo">SLO</Term> of 99.9% over 30 days allows 0.1% to fail: that's the **error budget**. As time,
0.1% of 30 days is about 43 minutes. 99.99% leaves about 4 minutes; 99% leaves about 7 hours.

<Depth>
  <ELI5>
    The SLO is a promise. The error budget is how much you're allowed to miss and still keep it.
    Spend it slowly and you're fine. Spend it fast and someone needs to act.
  </ELI5>
  <Operator>
    **Burn rate** is how fast you're spending it: a burn rate of 1 spends exactly the budget over
    the window; 14.4 spends 2% of a 30-day budget in an hour. Burn-rate alerts pair a long and a
    short window (1 hour and 5 minutes, say), so they page fast for a big burn and stop soon after
    it ends.
  </Operator>
</Depth>

</Step>

<Step id="conversations" title="Why 99.9% changes conversations">

Without an SLO, "is it reliable enough?" is an argument. With one, it's a number: if the budget is nearly
spent, the next release waits and reliability work comes first; if it's mostly left, ship. Nobody has to win
the argument at 3 a.m.

</Step>
