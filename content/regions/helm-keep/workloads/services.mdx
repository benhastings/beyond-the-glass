<Step id="moving-targets" title="Stable addresses for moving targets">

<Rosetta id="vip-service" />

Pods come and go, and each new one has a new IP address. A <Term id="service">Service</Term> gives a
group of pods one stable name and address. It finds its pods with a label selector, the same way a
Deployment does, and sends each request to one of the pods that are **Ready**.

</Step>

<Step id="endpoints" title="Endpoints">

<Depth>
  <ELI5>
    A Service keeps a list of the pods that are ready to answer. That list is called its endpoints. If
    the list is empty, nobody answers.
  </ELI5>
  <Operator>
    The endpoints are the IPs and ports of the pods the selector matches **and** that are Ready. `kubectl
    get endpoints NAME` shows them. An empty list (`<none>`) means the selector matches no pods, or none
    of them is Ready: requests to the Service fail even though pods may be running.
  </Operator>
</Depth>

</Step>

<Step id="dns" title="Names inside the cluster">

Inside the cluster, a Service is reachable by name. From a pod in the same namespace, `ravenpost-api`
is enough; from another namespace, `ravenpost-api.ravenpost`; the full name is
`ravenpost-api.ravenpost.svc.cluster.local`. Programs are configured with these names, never with pod IPs.

<Callout kind="info">
  When a pod can't resolve a name, the error is DNS-shaped: "no such host". You saw one in the
  notifier's logs: it was looking for a relay outside the cluster that doesn't exist.
</Callout>

</Step>
