<Step id="chain" title="Deployment, ReplicaSet, pods">

<Rosetta id="init-script-deployment" />

A <Term id="deployment">Deployment</Term> says what should run: which image, how many copies. It doesn't
make pods itself. It makes a **ReplicaSet** for each version, and the ReplicaSet keeps the right number of
pods. That's why a pod's name has two random parts: `ravenpost-web-74d9fdfcd5-252jk` is a pod of
ReplicaSet `ravenpost-web-74d9fdfcd5`.

<CodeBlock
  language="console"
  caption="The whole chain, in one list"
  code="kubectl get deploy,rs,pods -n ravenpost -l app=ravenpost-web"
/>

</Step>

<Step id="scale" title="Scaling">

`kubectl scale` changes the number of copies. The ReplicaSet adds or removes pods to match.

<Rosetta id="busy-season-scaling" />

<Callout kind="habit">
  On a GitOps-managed cluster, Git holds the desired state, so a hand-made scale is put back at the
  next sync. Here, in Helm Keep, nothing syncs from Git; in Mirror Lake you'll change it the lasting
  way.
</Callout>

</Step>

<Step id="rollout" title="Rolling updates">

Change the image, and the Deployment makes a new ReplicaSet. It brings new pods up a few at a time, and
takes old ones down only as new ones become ready, so the service keeps answering throughout.
`kubectl rollout status` waits and reports progress; `kubectl rollout history` lists the versions, and
`kubectl rollout undo` goes back one.

<Depth>
  <ELI5>
    The cluster swaps old copies for new ones a few at a time. There is always a copy ready to
    answer. If the new version is bad, you can switch back.
  </ELI5>
  <Operator>
    The default strategy is RollingUpdate, with `maxSurge` and `maxUnavailable` at 25%. A new pod
    counts only once it's Ready, so a version that never gets Ready stalls the rollout instead of
    finishing it. The `Recreate` strategy removes every old pod first, which means downtime.
  </Operator>
</Depth>

</Step>
