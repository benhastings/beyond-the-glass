<Step id="where-you-are" title="Read-only first">

<Analogy
  then="You opened the monitoring console and scanned the host list for anything red, before you touched a thing."
  now="You ask the cluster for its pods and read their status, before you change anything."
/>

Ravenpost, the mail service you look after, runs in a Kubernetes cluster. Each part of it runs in

<Term id="pod">pods</Term>, and Ravenpost's pods are grouped in a
<Term id="namespace">namespace</Term>
called `ravenpost`.

The commands in this lesson only read. You can run them as often as you like, anywhere, and change
nothing. That's what makes them the right first move.

</Step>

<Step id="ask" title="Ask the cluster">

<Depth>
  <ELI5>
    A namespace is a folder for a team's things. `kubectl get pods -n ravenpost` lists the pods in
    the ravenpost folder. Add `-A` to look in every folder at once.
  </ELI5>
  <Operator>
    `kubectl get pods -n ravenpost` lists the pods in one namespace; `-A` (all namespaces) lists
    every pod in the cluster, with a NAMESPACE column. READY shows ready containers out of the
    total, and STATUS shows where each pod is in its life. `-o wide` adds the node and IP; `-o yaml`
    prints the whole object.
  </Operator>
  <DeepDive>
    kubectl sends `GET /api/v1/namespaces/ravenpost/pods` (or `/api/v1/pods` for `-A`) and prints
    the server's table view. STATUS is a summary: the pod's phase, or the reason a container is
    waiting or terminated (CrashLoopBackOff, ImagePullBackOff, OOMKilled).
  </DeepDive>
</Depth>

<CodeBlock
  language="console"
  caption="Ravenpost's pods, and everything else's"
  code={`kubectl get pods -n ravenpost
kubectl get pods -A
kubectl get pods -n ravenpost -o wide`}
/>

<Callout kind="habit">
  Before you look, know where you're looking: the prompt shows the cluster and namespace you're
  pointed at.
</Callout>

</Step>

<Step id="find-it" title="Find the one that isn't running">

Mail isn't going out. List the pods in the `ravenpost` namespace and find the one that isn't Running.
When you know which it is, answer with `answer failing-pod <name>` (the start of the name is enough).

<Terminal scenario="ravenpost" challenge="find-the-failing-pod" required />

</Step>

<Step id="why" title="Then find out why">

A pod in <Term id="crashloopbackoff">CrashLoopBackOff</Term> keeps starting and exiting. Three
read-only commands tell you why:

<CodeBlock
  language="console"
  caption="State, events, and the last crashed run's logs"
  code={`kubectl describe pod ravenpost-notifier -n ravenpost
kubectl get events -n ravenpost --sort-by=.lastTimestamp
kubectl logs deploy/ravenpost-notifier -n ravenpost --previous`}
/>

`describe` shows **State** and **Last State** (with the exit code) and the pod's events. `--previous`
shows the <Term id="logs">logs</Term> of the run that crashed, not the one that's starting, which is
where the reason usually is.

<Terminal scenario="ravenpost" challenge="why-is-it-crashing" />

</Step>
