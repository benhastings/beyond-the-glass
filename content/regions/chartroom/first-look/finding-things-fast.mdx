<Step id="narrow" title="Narrow it, or widen it">

`-l` picks objects by label, so `-l app=ravenpost-api` is every api pod, whatever they're called today.
`-A` goes the other way: every namespace at once.

<CodeBlock
  language="console"
  caption="One app's pods, and every pod everywhere"
  code={`kubectl get pods -n ravenpost -l app=ravenpost-api
kubectl get pods -A`}
/>

</Step>

<Step id="sort" title="Sort what matters to the top">

`--sort-by` takes a path into the object. Two that earn their keep:

<CodeBlock
  language="console"
  caption="Newest events last; most restarted pods last"
  code={`kubectl get events -n ravenpost --sort-by=.lastTimestamp
kubectl get pods -A --sort-by=.status.containerStatuses[0].restartCount`}
/>

<Depth>
  <ELI5>
    Sorting puts things in order. Sort the events by time, and the newest is at the bottom. Sort
    pods by restarts, and the troublemaker is at the bottom.
  </ELI5>
  <Operator>
    The path is a JSONPath into each item, as `-o yaml` shows it. Sorting is ascending, so the
    newest or biggest is last, right above your prompt. Events without sorting come in no useful
    order.
  </Operator>
</Depth>

</Step>

<Step id="pipes" title="grep and head">

The terminal pipes kubectl's output through `grep`, `head`, `tail` and `wc -l`, as a shell would.

<CodeBlock
  language="console"
  caption="Only the lines you want"
  code={`kubectl get pods -A | grep -v Running
kubectl get events -n ravenpost --sort-by=.lastTimestamp | tail -5
kubectl get pods -n kube-system --no-headers | wc -l`}
/>

<Terminal scenario="ravenpost" challenge="most-restarts" required />

</Step>
